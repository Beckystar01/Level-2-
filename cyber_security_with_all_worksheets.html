📚 Unit 1: Introduction to Cyber Security
🔓 What is Cybercrime?

Definition: Criminal activity carried out using computers or the internet

Examples:

Identity theft - stealing personal information
Phishing - fraudulent emails to trick users
Ransomware - encrypting data until ransom paid
Data breaches - unauthorized access to systems
Online fraud - scams and deception
🕵️ Threat Actors: Who Attacks?

Five types of threat actors:

👤 Individual

Lone hackers motivated by curiosity, money, or revenge

🏢 Company

Business competitors engaging in espionage

🌐 Nation-State

Governments with massive resources

⚡ Hacker

Skilled technical attackers

✊ Hacktivist

Hackers with political/social motivations

🎯 Who Gets Targeted?

Primary Targets: Small & Medium Enterprises (SMEs)

Why? Limited budgets, can't afford security experts, more likely to pay ransoms (£1000+), weaker defenses

💰 Attack Motivations
Financial Gain - Ransomware, theft, fraud
Political - Influence politics, disrupt governments
Revenge - Disgruntled employees, feuds
Challenge - The thrill of breaking in
Business Competition - Stealing secrets, sabotage
Cyber Warfare - Nations conducting digital operations
🏛️ UK Cyber Defence

NCSC: National Cyber Security Centre - Education, Certification, Incident Response

GCHQ: Government Communications Headquarters - Signals intelligence & cyber security

ICO: Information Commissioner's Office - Data protection regulation (HQ Wilmslow, founded 1984)

📚 Unit 4: Malicious Software, Physical Threats & IoT
🦠 What is Malicious Software?

Malware: Any program intentionally harmful to a computer, network or server.

Common types: Viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware, spyware

💰 Ransomware

Extortion software that locks your computer and demands a ransom for its release.

🦠 Virus

Attaches to another program, replicates and spreads when first run.

🕵️ Spyware

Software that covertly monitors your online behaviour without permission.

🐴 Trojan

Appears as harmless software but contains malicious code hidden inside.

🪱 Worm

Self-replicating malware that spreads automatically across networks.

🛡️ Perimeter Access Control

Systems that protect and monitor the external boundary of a business.

🔥 Firewall & Network Defenses

Firewall: Filters traffic with rules | IDS: Detects threats | IPS: Blocks threats | Border Router: Manages network edge

🌐 Internet of Things (IoT)

Network of physical devices with sensors and connectivity.

Examples: CCTV, ID scanners, sensors, access controls

✅ IoT Benefits & Risks

Benefits: Efficiency, safety, data insights, communication

Risks: Vulnerable to attacks, weak credentials, lack of updates

📚 Unit 5: Maintaining Cyber Security, Testing & Firewalls
🛡️ Preventative Methods Overview

Several methods protect against digital attacks:

Security updates & patching
Antivirus, firewalls, network segmentation
Encryption & data backup
User education & password policies
Network monitoring & penetration testing
🔄 Security Updates & Patching

Keep systems up-to-date with patches. Patches fix security vulnerabilities and bugs.

🦠 Antivirus Software

Detects, prevents, and removes malware. Keep it updated against latest threats.

🔥 Firewalls

Control traffic flowing in/out of a network. Prevent unauthorised access.

🌐 Network Segmentation

Divide network into smaller segments. Limits threat exposure.

🔐 Encryption & Backup

Encryption: Encode data so only authorized users can read it

Backup: Copies of data to restore after breach or failure

👥 User Education

The biggest cyber risk is internal staff via phishing or negligence!

🔑 Password Policies & Testing

Password Policy: Rules for strong passwords

Penetration Testing: Attempt to exploit vulnerabilities to test security
